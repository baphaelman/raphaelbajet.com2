        Self-improving bots

        Tunes a bot's weights for the rules on the left. Each step nudges every weight up or down at random, giving a nudged-up and a nudged-down bot; both play the same pairs of games (same openings, colours swapped) against a field of other bots, and the weights move toward whichever scored more. Every few steps it plays the opponent you pick below at the same look-ahead, to measure progress in Elo. Each bot keeps its own tuning run, so you can switch between them.

        Games run on the fast engine (WebAssembly) whenever it can play both bots: the same games the JavaScript bots would play, move for move, about five times faster. On the 7×7 mandatory-fill rules a minute of tuning gains about +55 to +80 Elo at look-ahead 2 (most of it in the first ten seconds), +70 at look-ahead 3 and +86 in 2½ minutes at look-ahead 4; one more move of look-ahead is worth +70 to +160 there. The weights alone level off within a minute or two: the training-score chart shows when. Looking further while fills are on offer (3 more moves) is worth nearly a whole move of look-ahead at a fraction of its cost, and tuning on top of it took the Accountant at look-ahead 2 to +94 over Default at look-ahead 3. Tuned bots are not equally better against every style, so a ladder check plays the same panel of bots every time.

        A pattern bot is not tuned by nudging weights: it has hundreds of thousands of table entries, one per small pattern (every 3×3 neighbourhood and every 6-cell row, column or diagonal, read as empty, the mover's, the opponent's or wall). Each step, every worker plays games against itself on the rules on the left, choosing moves one ply deep, and moves each pattern's value toward how the game actually went; then the page merges what the workers learned. The progress checks and saving work as for the other bots. The shipped table took 1.3 million games (4 minutes on 6 cores) and is about +180 over Default at look-ahead 3 on the 7×7 mandatory-fill rules; on rules it never saw (orthogonal lines, "fewest wins", other placements) start from it or from an empty table and let it learn.

        Every check replays the same openings, so two checks differ only because the weights changed. The openings are still a sample: 200 pairs put the whole curve within about ±35 Elo of the truth, the same at every check, so read its shape and rate a bot properly when you save it. More pairs per step and a bigger step size are not better: in tests, step size 1 or bigger nudges halved the gain.

        Try weights at other look-aheads or on other rules

        Plays a bot against an opponent at each look-ahead you list, on the rules on the left. The bot can be the weights being tuned here or any saved bot, including bots tuned on another board size, at another look-ahead or for other rules: switch the rules first to try them there.         Saved bots

        Saving rates the new bot. When these rules have a ladder, it plays the ladder bots nearest its strength (two rounds, the second re-centred on the first) and gets a ladder Elo, the same as rating it on the Ladder tab. Without a ladder it plays Minimax (Default) at the same look-ahead, so every Elo here means "points above the default bot at that look-ahead". To put it on one scale with every other bot, rate it on the tab. Saved bots appear in the player and Lab menus.

        Bot ladder

        One standard for Elo. Every bot here played every other in pairs of games from the same random first move, colours swapped, and the ratings are the single set of numbers that best explains all of those results (a Bradley–Terry fit). Random is pinned at 0, so a rating never drifts: 400 points apart means the stronger bot is expected to score about 91%.

        You on this ladder

        Games you play to the end against one bot on the Play tab, from the normal start, without take-backs, paused bots or move scores. Your rating is fitted with the bots' ratings held fixed, just as a new bot joins the ladder, and recent games count more (a game counts half once you have played 40 more), so it follows you as you improve.

        Your bots on this ladder

        Bots you saved on the Tune tab for these exact rules. Rating one plays it against the frozen bots nearest its strength and fits its rating with theirs held fixed, so it lands on the same scale without moving anyone else. Until then the number shown is a guess: the ladder rating of Default at the same look-ahead plus the bot's Tune-tab Elo.

        Head to head

        Each cell is the row bot's score against the column bot (wins plus half the draws). Blue: the row bot won most; orange: it lost most.

        A ladder for other rules

        Tuning on some rules doesn't make a ladder for them: only ysbvl comes with one. For the rules in the panel on the left you can build your own: the quicker bots (and your saved bots for those rules) play a round robin here in the browser. It is kept in this browser only.
